Video Stitching

Developed a video stitching solution for user‑generated content (UGC) recorded with handheld cameras at large venues. The system aligned and merged multiple video streams from different users, solving synchronization, stabilization, and color mismatch challenges to produce seamless stitched outputs with improved speed and quality.

Video Stitching UGC Handheld Cameras Synchronization Stabilization Color Transfer Computational Optimization Image Quality

Project Description

We designed and implemented a robust stitching algorithm capable of handling a large number of videos recorded simultaneously by individual users on mobile devices. The solution addressed key challenges such as time synchronization, camera sensor mismatch, and video stabilization, ensuring smooth stitched outputs even with diverse sources.

Key Features Delivered:
  • Audio‑based time synchronization across multiple handheld video streams
  • Video stabilization to reduce jitter and handheld shake
  • Color transfer methods to correct sensor mismatch between different devices
  • Optimized transformation matrix handling to avoid repeated calculations
  • Seamless stitching of large numbers of videos into a unified output
  • Improved computation speed and subjective image quality compared to screen stitching approaches

In practice, the system demonstrated superior performance both in terms of processing efficiency and visual quality making it suitable for large‑scale event coverage where multiple users contribute video content.

Stitching Videos Taken By Unaligned Handheld Cameras

Naver Paper of the Industry Award Winner The paper presents a fast video stitching algorithm for fixed or slowly moving multiple videos. Video stitching from unaligned capturing devices is challenging process as it requires synchronization in time, spatial, and color domain. Correlated information should be used to overcome the computational load in frame-by-frame stitching. The proposed stabilization algorithm obtains in-video motions of each video and applies them into registration information in stitching process to avoid frame-by-frame re-registration. The experiments show the proposed algorithm achieves very small PSNR degradation than frame-by-frame stitching.

2

Stitching Method of Videos Recorded by Multiple Handheld Cameras

This Paper Presents a Method for Stitching a Large Number of Images Recorded by a Large Number of Individual Users Through a Cellular Phone Camera at a Venue. In Contrast to 360 Camera Solutions that Use Existing Fixed Rigs, these Conditions must Address New Challenges Such as Time Synchronization, Repeated Transformation Matrix Calculations, and Camera Sensor Mismatch Correction. In this Paper, we Solve this Problem by Updating the Transformation Matrix Using Time Synchronization Method Using Audio, Sensor Mismatch Removal by Color Transfer Method, and Global Operation Stabilization Algorithm. Experimental Results Show that the Proposed Algorithm Shows better Performance in Terms of Computation Speed and Subjective Image Quality than that of Screen Stitching.

Tools & Technologies

The solution was built using C++ and OpenCV, leveraging advanced computer vision techniques for stabilization, synchronization, and color correction. The architecture emphasized computational efficiency, minimizing redundant calculations while maintaining high subjective image quality. This ensured scalability for large datasets of user‑generated videos and adaptability across diverse mobile camera hardware.
