Virtual Try-On System

The Multi‑Layer Try‑On Pipeline is an advanced AI‑driven system that enables realistic virtual clothing visualization. It integrates segmentation, warping, and inpainting networks to simulate how garments fit and interact with human body poses. The pipeline supports both tuck‑in and tuck‑out views, ensuring natural layering and high visual fidelity for commercial fashion applications.

Virtual Try‑On Cloth Warping Image Inpainting Pose Estimation CIHP‑PGN Segmentation Layered Clothing Simulation Computer Vision Deep Learning Fashion Technology

Project Description

We designed and implemented a multi‑stage pipeline that accurately models how clothes appear on a person under different configurations. The system begins with human body pose estimation to determine the spatial orientation of the model. Using CIHP‑PGN segmentation, we identify and separate clothing layers — outerwear, innerwear, and bottoms — ensuring precise control over each garment’s position and overlap.

Both preserved‑mask and mask‑less operations were explored for cloth warping, allowing flexibility in handling complex textures and shapes. The pipeline follows a logical sequence: detecting body pose, layering garments, performing warping transformations, and finally applying an inpainting network to refine edges and fill occluded regions for photorealistic output.

Key Features Delivered:
  • Multi‑layer garment management (outer, inner, bottom)
  • Tuck‑in and tuck‑out view generation
  • Pose‑aware cloth alignment
  • CIHP‑PGN segmentation for precise part detection
  • Mask‑based and mask‑less warping modes
  • Inpainting for seamless visual correction
  • Almost commercial‑grade visual quality

The project resulted in a core algorithm delivered to a partner company for commercialization. It served as the foundation for their virtual try‑on product line. A technical paper was prepared detailing the integration of segmentation, warping, and inpainting networks, and the project received internal certification for innovation in AI‑based fashion visualization.

Tools & Technologies

The pipeline combines deep learning, computer vision, and image synthesis techniques. We used PyTorch for warping model training and inference, OpenPose for body pose estimation, and Flask for backend integration. The system was deployed through a modular architecture that supports real‑time visualization and scalable commercial use.
